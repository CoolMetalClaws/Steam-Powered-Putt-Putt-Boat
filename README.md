# Steam Powered Putt Putt Boat

![Alt text](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019f31d0-046e-7cd2-97fc-c8a72bc24924/screenshot_2026-07-05_181443.png)

## Overview

A 3D Printed, steam powered boat that uses parts of a drinks can to create a steam powered boiler engine for a mini putt putt boat toy. The boat is designed to run in water using a simple tealight candle. 

## Features

- Hydrodynamic design for a buoyant and fast moving boat
- Easy to assemble and requires minimal hardware
- Quick to print and assemble, using minimal time and resources
- Detachable boiler for easy assembly and ease of use
- Fully 3D printed body
- Compact steam powered watercraft
- Removable fuel supply

## 3D Printing Settings

### Material :

PLA or PETG are best suited for this project, though most fillaments should work perfectly. No specific fillaments are required. Ensure fillament is completely dry before printing to ensure part sizing and fit, as bubbling may cause an uneven surface.

### Infill : 

10% infill recommended. Less infill should be okay, but nothing below 5% infill. Recommended infill type is Gyroid or Tri-Hexagon infill for higher strength.

### Outer Walls : 

2 outer walls recommended as the part is meant to be under constant use, but cannot have excess weight.

### Supports : 

No supports are required. Please print both parts flat on bed in the orientation shown below.

## Assembly and Use Instructions

### 1. 

Clean up the 3D printed parts (Remove stringing if visible) and test fitting. If parts are too loose, use 1 layer of masking tape around the insertion point for a tighter fit.

### 2. 

Follow the steps below to create a boiler using a coke can and the hardware listed above.

   #### Items required:

   ![Alt text](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019f36e1-372f-7460-bc6c-fc50c36ca0a5/use_ai_image_jul_6__2026__20_01_36.png)

   1) Cut open soda can using the exacto knife and scissors as shown in the image below. Do not discard any parts.

   ![Alt text](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019f36e8-466b-7269-99be-d0f5fcc5f4f1/image12.jpg)

   2) Trim down sharp edges, being careful not to cut yourself. trim excess material from the concave shaped part from the base of the can.

   ![Alt text](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019f36ea-e76d-7631-8bfb-31030e5da05f/image14.jpg)

   3) Using a hammer, make a concave dent on the base of the can.

   ![Alt text](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019f36ec-2b06-7e83-a576-a1e880d117ab/image5.jpg)

   4) Using the input side of the boiler exhaust tube, measure out and cut a flap where the the part fits snugly. Bend flap open and tes the fit of the 3D printed exhaust tube.

   ![Alt text](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019f36ed-a173-7fb9-a60f-4621828ab5ef/image11.jpg)

   5) Cut a roughly sized peice of the can to seal of the bottom of the boiler. OPTIONAL: If the sheet of can continues to flex, tape down edges with masking tape to hold in place.

   ![Alt text](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019f36ef-ba39-7a5d-b2cd-b41d2750013d/image15.jpg)

   6) Seal off all edges of the boiler with epoxy adhesive.

   7) Once the epoxy has cured partially, trim down edges.(I have temporarily used masking tape to hold the parts together, as seen in the image)

   ![Alt text](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019f36f2-77c4-7cd5-8053-6f6cffa6b48e/image13.jpg)

   8) Add the 3D printed exhaust in, with the boiler angled slightly upwards when attached to the exhaust. Use the epoxy to seal around the 3D printed exhaust, closing all gaps.

   ![Alt text](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019f36f4-f07e-7e0b-b748-b6843af70347/image1.jpg)

   9) Once the epoxy has fully set, check for leaks. If any leaks are found, use more epoxy to seal all leaks.

   10) Once the adhesive has fully set, the boiler is ready to use!

   ![Alt text](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019f36f5-de4c-7380-b2f1-fe9753aaf57d/image16.jpg)


### 3. 

Follow the steps below to create a removable fuel holder using the remainder of the coke can and the hardware listed above.


 1) Cut the remaning sheet from soda can using the scissors as shown in the image below with the approx dimensions of 35 x 95 mm 

    ![Alt text](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019f3710-ca6c-7121-a953-79fa98574101/image4.jpg)

 2) Fold over the top corners, making sure not to cut yourself

    ![Alt text](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019f3716-c0c4-7e1e-be95-c2f96d68cdea/image2.jpg)

 3) Fold in the sides of the sheet, with the bottom corners being closer. refer to the image below.

    ![Alt text](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019f3719-bc19-7571-b28f-534e24b7bac7/image8.jpg)

 4) Fold the handle part in half and angle upwards. refer to the image below.

    ![Alt text](https://cdn.hackclub.com/019f371d-c76c-7fd4-bce3-e467166afdb6/image7.jpg)

 5) The Aluminum can fuel holder is ready to use!



#### 4. Fill the boiler with water using either a syringe or a pipette and connect to boat. Ensure there is a large enough gap to insert fuel so it doesn't extinguish

#### 5. Prepare fuel. fuel can be anything that can sustain a stable flame beneath the boiler. MY METHOD: Melt candle wax into cavity in the fuel holder. While the wax is still wet, press in a singular piece of camphor. 

#### 6. Place boat in water and hold in place. 

#### 7. Light fuel and place in boat, being careful not to extinguish the flame. 

#### 8. Your boat is ready! Watch it slowly putt putt away into the distance! (The boiler may take up to 3 minutes to fully heat up)
